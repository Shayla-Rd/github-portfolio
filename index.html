MAE 3230 — Fluid Mechanics Project

Instructional Video: Fluid Dynamics of a Gas-Powered Chainsaw

As part of MAE 3230 (Fluid Mechanics), I completed a technical project in which my team selected a mechanical system and produced an instructional video explaining its fluid-dynamic behavior. Our assigned system was a gas-powered chainsaw. I led the mechanical teardown, removing the external housing and isolating individual subsystems for analysis.

My primary technical contribution focused on the rotating flow generated by the flywheel. After separating the flywheel from the crankshaft assembly, I evaluated how the blades act as a centrifugal impeller and how angular momentum is converted into useful airflow. Because the flywheel is rigidly mounted on the crankshaft, its rotational speed matches the piston cycle, and the blade geometry sets up outward flow across the engine.

From a fluid-mechanics standpoint, the air experiences constant mass flow as radius increases, but the changing flow area causes radial velocity to decrease while the tangential component grows with blade speed. This produces a net rise in fluid kinetic energy, and the corresponding pressure increase reflects the mechanical work transferred into the flow. That outward spiral delivers forced convection over the cylinder fins and ignition components, preventing thermal failure during extended operation.

This project helped solidify several concepts: how a rotating device transfers shaft work to a fluid, why impeller geometry affects velocity components, and how increases in kinetic energy translate into cooling capacity. More broadly, taking the hardware apart made control-volume analysis, energy balance, and momentum transfer feel less abstract, and showed how small consumer machines hide multiple fluid subsystems in a compact package.
