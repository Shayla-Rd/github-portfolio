MAE 3230 — Fluid Mechanics

Chainsaw Engine Fluid Mechanical Dissection

As part of MAE 3230 (Fluid Mechanics), I completed a technical project in which my team selected a mechanical system and produced an instructional video explaining its fluid-dynamic behavior. Our assigned system was a gas-powered chainsaw. I led the mechanical teardown, removing the external housing and isolating individual subsystems for analysis.

My primary technical contribution focused on the rotating flow generated by the flywheel. After separating the flywheel from the crankshaft assembly, I evaluated how the blades act as a centrifugal impeller and how angular momentum is converted into useful airflow. Because the flywheel is rigidly mounted on the crankshaft, its rotational speed matches the piston cycle, and the blade geometry sets up outward flow across the engine. From a fluid-mechanics standpoint, the air experiences constant mass flow as radius increases, but the changing flow area causes radial velocity to decrease while the tangential component grows with blade speed. This produces a net rise in fluid kinetic energy, and the corresponding pressure increase reflects the mechanical work transferred into the flow. That outward spiral delivers forced convection over the cylinder fins and ignition components, preventing thermal failure during extended operation.

This project helped solidify several concepts: how a rotating device transfers shaft work to a fluid, why impeller geometry affects velocity components, and how increases in kinetic energy translate into cooling capacity. More broadly, taking the hardware apart made control-volume analysis, energy balance, and momentum transfer feel less abstract, and showed how small consumer machines hide multiple fluid subsystems in a compact package.

MAE 3260 — System Dynamics

Analysis of System Dynamics of a Toy Quadcopter

For this project, my team analyzed the system dynamics of a small quadcopter by developing a model that links motor voltage to propeller speed, thrust, and altitude. Since the drone lacks onboard sensors and internal stabilization, we treated it as a fully open-loop system and relied on experimental data to identify its parameters. Using a high-speed camera, I recorded the propeller’s step response and extracted speed and timing data to estimate the motor gain and time constant. Through this work, I gained experience in system identification, control modeling, and interpreting real experimental data.

MAE 3270 — Mechanical Design

Torque Wrench Design & Analysis

I worked with a partner to design a torque wrench that could withstand a fully reversed loading of ±600 in-lbf while meeting strict performance and safety requirements. We used iterative design scripts in MATLAB to explore geometries and materials, followed by a detailed CAD model and finite element stress analyses in ANSYS. This allowed us to verify that the design met all static, fatigue, and fracture criteria and to compare FEM results with our hand calculations.
